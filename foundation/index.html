FOUNDATION / OPEN SOURCE / COMMUNITY

BUILT TOGETHER.
OPEN BY PURPOSE.

Free creative tools. A community that can shape them. SHOWAVE is committed to fully free and open-source software, sustained by the people who build, use and support it.

Our purposePeople & decisionsSupport the workSource & participation

Creative freedom is the purpose.

SHOWAVE is a project by Afro Unity Group. Its foundation initiative brings software development, practical learning and community participation into one shared effort.

01 / FREEDOM

Tools people can shape.

Our commitment is software people can use, study, change and share. Source access, a published license and build instructions belong alongside each open-source release.

02 / PARTICIPATION

More than code.

Developers, artists, operators, educators, writers and testers all help improve the tools. Clear feedback and a useful guide are contributions too.

03 / CONTINUITY

Keep the work moving.

Voluntary support gives people time to fix problems, test productions, document workflows and maintain the shared infrastructure.

Different roles. A shared responsibility.

This is the working stewardship model for SHOWAVE. Roles follow the work, and decisions should carry a clear explanation.

FOUNDING / PURPOSE

Keep the mission clear.

Godrick Sankofa founded SHOWAVE. Afro Unity Group is the project home. Founding stewardship carries the project’s purpose and helps create the conditions for contributors to succeed.

CONTRIBUTORS / EXPERIENCE

Bring the real workflow.

Contributors propose improvements, share designs, write documentation and test changes. Participation is open to different skills, with named or anonymous recognition by choice.

MAINTAINERS / QUALITY

Look after each area.

Maintainers coordinate work, review changes, explain priorities and check tests and documentation before release. Maintainer appointments and responsibilities should be recorded as the team develops.

Make the path from idea to release visible.

Our intended workflow follows open-source practice: discuss the need, develop a focused change, review it with the people affected, then test and document the result.

01 / DISCUSS

Start with the problem.

Describe a production need or a reproducible bug. Keep the proposal and its rationale together so others can understand the decision.

Project issues · access required ↗
02 / REVIEW

Build in focused steps.

Propose a small change with a test, screenshot or example. Maintainers review it, contributors improve it, and affected users help check the outcome.

Pull requests · access required ↗
03 / RELEASE

Share the evidence.

Stabilize the work, document its limits and publish release notes. A preview is a place to learn; a stable release must meet its quality gates.

Follow the roadmap ↗

Back the tools your community needs.

Support does not buy equity, guaranteed financial returns or control of the roadmap. Sponsor recognition does not imply endorsement of a sponsor’s products. Downloads remain available without a donation.

Trust needs a visible record.

Our reporting commitment is to publish funding received, how it is used, supported work and progress when verified records are available. Contributor activity and financial reporting are separate records.
