FROM AN IDEA TO A SHARED IMPROVEMENT

Small changes.
Thoughtful review.

Our contribution workflow starts with the problem and ends with something another person can understand, test and use.

  1. 01

    Discuss.

    Describe the need and check existing work. Agree larger features or changes in direction before implementation.

  2. 02

    Build.

    Keep the change focused. Follow the project guidance and explain the choices a reviewer needs to understand.

  3. 03

    Review.

    Ask the relevant maintainer to review the work. Share context, respond to feedback and revise together.

  4. 04

    Test & document.

    Show the result, check affected workflows and update the guide. Record limitations that remain.

  5. 05

    Release & credit.

    A maintainer decides when to merge. Record the change for a release and confirm how contributors want to be credited.

Start with the scope. A small correction can go straight to a focused review. Larger changes need an agreed direction and a written discussion others can follow.

SHARED RESPONSIBILITY

A clear place for each decision.

These are the responsibilities we are organizing around. They describe the working model; module teams and named maintainer appointments will be published as they are established.

CONTRIBUTORS

Bring the context.

Explain the problem, prepare a focused contribution and take part in review. First-time and non-code contributors are equally welcome.

MODULE MAINTAINERS

Care for an area.

Help agree scope, review changes and keep an area coherent. Start with the workspace or guide your contribution affects.

RELEASE STEWARDS

Check what is ready.

Coordinate release checks, known issues and clear notes so operators understand the build they are downloading.

PROJECT STEWARDSHIP

Keep decisions visible.

Document project direction, responsibilities and funding decisions, with space for community feedback.

Start in the area you know: Live & Broadcast, lighting & control, media preparation, mapping & outputs, or learning & community.
